# Model answer — A Time You Were Late

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

I'd like to talk about a time I was late for a job interview, which was probably one of the most stressful mornings of my life. It was about two years ago, when I was applying for my current job.

The interview was at ten o'clock in the city centre, and I'd planned to take a train that would get me there about thirty minutes early. Unfortunately, there was a signal failure on the line, and the train sat outside a station for nearly forty minutes. To make matters worse, my phone battery had died because I'd forgotten to charge it the night before, so I couldn't even call to let them know.

When the train finally arrived, I ran all the way from the station and turned up about twenty-five minutes late, out of breath and very embarrassed. I apologised and explained what had happened, and luckily the interviewer was very understanding. She said she'd seen the news about the train delays and had rearranged her schedule to wait for me.

I felt awful at the time — I was convinced I'd ruined my chances. But in the end, the interview went well, and I got the job. The interviewer later told me that the way I'd stayed calm and explained the situation honestly had actually made a good impression.

Since then, I've become slightly obsessive about punctuality. For anything important, I always leave at least an hour early and make sure my phone is fully charged.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Why are some people often late?**

> Some people genuinely underestimate how long things take, while others try to fit too many tasks into their day. There's also a personality element — some people simply don't see punctuality as a priority.

**Is punctuality more important in some cultures than others?**

> Definitely. In countries like Germany or Japan, being even a few minutes late can be seen as disrespectful, whereas in many other cultures, timings for social events are much more flexible.

**How has technology changed the way we manage time?**

> It's made it easier in some ways — we have calendar reminders and real-time traffic updates. But it also means we can message people to say we're running late, which may have made people less careful about being on time.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
