# Model answer — A Person Who Influenced You

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

I'd like to talk about my maths teacher from secondary school, Mrs Menon, who taught me when I was around fourteen. I haven't seen her for several years now, but I still think of her often.

At the time, I was really struggling with maths. I'd convinced myself that I just wasn't a 'numbers person', and my marks were getting worse every term. Mrs Menon noticed this, and instead of simply giving me lower grades, she asked me to stay behind after class a couple of times a week. She'd go through problems with me step by step, and she never made me feel stupid for asking basic questions.

What I remember most about her is the combination of patience and high expectations. She was very kind, and she had a great sense of humour, but she also refused to accept excuses. If I said, 'I can't do this', she'd say, 'You can't do it yet.' That small word 'yet' really stuck with me.

By the end of the year, I'd gone from nearly failing to getting one of the top marks in the class. But the reason she influenced me so much isn't really about maths. She taught me that ability isn't fixed — that most things can be learned through steady effort. I've applied that lesson to everything since, from learning to code to preparing for this exam. So I'd say she shaped not just my results, but my whole attitude towards learning.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Who usually has more influence on children — parents or teachers?**

> I'd say parents generally have the greater influence, simply because children spend far more time with them, especially in the early years when values and habits are formed. That said, teachers can be extremely important during adolescence, when teenagers sometimes listen to adults outside the family more than to their own parents.

**Do you think celebrities are good role models for young people?**

> It really depends on the celebrity. Some athletes, for example, demonstrate discipline and resilience, which is a positive message. But a lot of celebrity culture focuses on wealth and appearance, which can give young people unrealistic expectations. So I think it's important that young people also have role models in their own lives.

**How has the role of teachers changed in recent years?**

> I think teachers have moved from being the main source of information to being more like guides. Since students can find facts online in seconds, a good teacher now needs to help them evaluate information, think critically and stay motivated, rather than simply delivering content.

## Useful phrases

- *stuck with me*
- *ability isn't fixed*
- *shaped my attitude towards …*
- *That said, …*
- *It really depends on …*

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
