# Model answer — A Festival or Celebration

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

I'd like to talk about Onam, which is the biggest festival in my home state of Kerala, in southern India. It's a harvest festival, and it's celebrated every year in August or September, depending on the traditional calendar.

The celebrations last for about ten days, and they build up to the main day, which is called Thiruvonam. One of the most beautiful traditions is the pookalam — a large, colourful circular design made entirely of flower petals, which families create in front of their houses. Each day the design gets bigger and more elaborate, and there are competitions for the best one.

The highlight for most people, though, is the sadya, a huge vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf. It can include more than twenty different dishes, and the whole family sits down together to eat it. There are also snake-boat races on the rivers, where long boats with around a hundred rowers compete, and the atmosphere is incredible.

I think Onam is important for a couple of reasons. Firstly, it's linked to a legend about a just and generous king, and it celebrates the idea of equality and prosperity. Secondly, and maybe more importantly today, it's celebrated by people of all religions and communities in Kerala, so it brings everyone together. For people like me who live away from home, it's also the time when families reunite, so it has a lot of emotional significance.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Why are traditional festivals important?**

> They help preserve a society's culture and history, and pass it on to younger generations. They also strengthen family and community ties, since people come together to prepare and celebrate.

**Do you think festivals have become too commercial?**

> To some extent, yes. Businesses use festivals as a chance to sell products, and there's pressure to buy gifts and new clothes. But I think the core traditions — the family meal, the rituals — are still meaningful for most people.

**How do young people's attitudes to festivals differ from older people's?**

> Older people often focus on religious or traditional aspects, while young people may see festivals more as social occasions and holidays. But I've noticed that many young people are rediscovering traditions, partly by sharing them on social media.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
