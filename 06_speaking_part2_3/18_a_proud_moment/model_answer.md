# Model answer — Something That Made You Proud

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

Something I'm really proud of is completing a ten-kilometre charity run last year. That might not sound like much to experienced runners, but for me it was a big achievement.

About six months before the race, I couldn't even run one kilometre without stopping. I'd become quite unfit after a long period of working from home, and I wanted to change that. A colleague mentioned that a local running club was training a group of beginners for a charity race, so I decided to join.

The training was much harder than I expected. We met three times a week, and the coach gradually increased the distance — from running for one minute and walking for two, to eventually running continuously. There were several times when I wanted to give up, especially in the hot weather, but the other members of the group were really encouraging.

At the same time, I was raising money for a children's hospital, and many of my colleagues and family members sponsored me. In the end, we raised about eighty thousand rupees, which was far more than my original target.

On the day of the race, I finished in just over an hour. It wasn't a fast time, but I didn't stop once, and crossing the finish line was an incredible feeling.

I felt proud for two reasons. First, because of the personal challenge — I'd gone from being unable to run a kilometre to completing ten. And second, because the money we raised was going to help children. It taught me that steady effort can achieve far more than I'd imagined.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Should parents praise their children a lot?**

> Praise is important for building confidence, but I think it should be specific and focused on effort rather than talent. Constant praise for everything can make it meaningless.

**What achievements are valued in your society?**

> Academic and professional success are very highly valued — getting into a good university or securing a well-paid job. Increasingly, though, people also admire those who start businesses or contribute to their communities.

**Is it better to work alone or in a team to achieve goals?**

> It depends on the goal. Teams are better for complex projects that require different skills, while individual work suits tasks that need deep concentration. For personal goals, like my run, having a supportive group made a huge difference.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
