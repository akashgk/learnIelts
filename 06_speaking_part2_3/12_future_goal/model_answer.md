# Model answer — A Goal for the Future

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

One goal I'd really like to achieve is to complete a Master's degree in public health at a university abroad — ideally in the UK or Canada — within the next two years.

I set this goal about two years ago. At the time, I was volunteering with a charity that organises health camps in rural villages, and I was shocked by how many people had problems that could easily have been prevented with basic information about hygiene, nutrition or vaccinations. It made me realise that I wanted to work on preventing illness, not just treating it.

There are several things I need to do to make this happen. The most immediate step is getting a score of at least 7.5 in IELTS, which is why I'm preparing so seriously at the moment. I also need to save money, because studying abroad is very expensive, so I'm putting aside a fixed amount from my salary every month and applying for scholarships. And I'm trying to get more research experience by helping with a small study at the charity.

The reason this goal is so important to me is that it combines personal ambition with something meaningful. I'd love to experience studying in an international environment, but more importantly, I want to bring back what I learn and help design better health programmes for rural communities in my own country. When I imagine looking back in twenty years, that's the kind of contribution I'd be proud to have made.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Is it important for young people to set goals?**

> Yes, I think goals give young people direction and motivation. However, it's also important that goals are flexible, because people's interests change, and being too rigid can lead to disappointment.

**What stops people from achieving their goals?**

> Common obstacles include a lack of time or money, but often the biggest barrier is psychological — fear of failure or a lack of confidence. Setting goals that are too vague or too ambitious can also make it hard to make progress.

**Do you think success is mainly due to hard work or luck?**

> I'd say it's a combination. Hard work is essential, but factors like the family you're born into, your education and timing play a significant role. Hard work increases your chances of being ready when luck comes along.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
