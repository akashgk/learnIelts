# Model answer — An Environmental Problem in Your Area

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

An environmental problem that really concerns me in my area is plastic pollution in the canals and backwaters around my hometown.

Kochi is surrounded by water — there are canals running through the city and a network of lagoons nearby — and in many places you can see plastic bottles, bags and food packaging floating on the surface or caught along the banks. After heavy rain, the problem becomes even more visible.

There are several causes. Single-use plastic is still widely used, especially for takeaway food and drinks. Waste collection in some neighbourhoods is irregular, so people sometimes throw rubbish into the canals simply because they have nowhere else to put it. Tourism also adds to the problem, since visitors often leave litter behind on houseboat trips.

The effects on people are significant. Plastic blocks the drains and canals, which makes flooding worse during the monsoon season. In some areas there's an unpleasant smell, and the pollution harms fish, which affects local fishing communities who depend on them for their income. It also spoils the natural beauty that attracts tourists in the first place.

I think several things could be done. The local government has already banned some single-use plastics, but the ban needs to be enforced more strictly. There should be more public bins and regular waste collection, along with recycling centres. Education is important too — schools and community groups could organise canal clean-ups, which would also raise awareness. If residents, businesses and the authorities worked together, I believe the waterways could be cleaned up within a few years.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Who should be responsible for protecting the environment?**

> Everyone has a role, but I think governments and businesses carry the greatest responsibility because they make decisions on a large scale. Individuals can contribute through their daily choices and by putting pressure on those in power.

**Are young people more environmentally aware than older people?**

> In general, I think they are, partly because environmental issues are taught in schools and discussed widely on social media. That said, many older people have long-standing habits of saving and reusing things that are very environmentally friendly.

**Can technology solve environmental problems?**

> It can certainly help — renewable energy and electric vehicles are good examples. But technology alone isn't enough; we also need to change how much we consume.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
