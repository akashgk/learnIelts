# Model answer — A Piece of Technology You Use

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

I'd like to talk about my noise-cancelling headphones. I know that might seem like a small thing, but I use them every single day.

I got them as a birthday present from my sister about two years ago. Before that, I'd always used cheap earphones, and I didn't really see the point of spending so much on headphones, but she insisted they'd make a difference — and she was right.

I use them for three main things. First, on my commute. The metro is often crowded and noisy, and with noise cancellation turned on, the sound of the train almost disappears, so I can listen to podcasts or audiobooks comfortably. Second, I use them at work. I work in an open-plan office, which can be quite distracting, so when I need to focus on something difficult, I put them on, sometimes without even playing music. And third, I use them for video calls, because the microphone is much clearer than my laptop's.

The way they've changed my life is mostly about concentration and stress. I used to arrive at work feeling tired from the noise of the journey, whereas now I arrive much calmer. I've also got through dozens of audiobooks on my commute, which means I'm 'reading' far more than I used to.

The only downside is that I sometimes forget to take them off and don't hear people talking to me! But overall, they've been one of the most useful things I own.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**How has technology changed the way people work?**

> Enormously. Many people can now work from anywhere, collaborate in real time with colleagues around the world, and automate repetitive tasks. On the other hand, technology has made it harder to switch off, because people are always reachable.

**Do you think older people find it hard to keep up with technology?**

> Some do, especially when devices and apps change so quickly. But I think it's often more about confidence than ability. With patient support, many older people become very comfortable with technology.

**Are there any disadvantages of relying too much on technology?**

> Yes — people can lose basic skills, like navigating without GPS or doing mental arithmetic. There's also the risk of addiction to screens and of privacy problems, since many devices collect large amounts of personal data.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
