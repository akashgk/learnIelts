# Model answer — An Old Family Object

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

The object I'd like to describe is a traditional brass oil lamp that has been in my family for over eighty years. In my language it's called a nilavilakku, and it's quite common in homes in Kerala, but ours is especially precious to us.

It was originally a wedding present to my great-grandmother, and it has been passed down from mother to daughter ever since. My grandmother gave it to my mother when she got married, and it now sits in a corner of our living room. It's about half a metre tall, with a round base and a small dish at the top where you put oil and cotton wicks. Over the years, the brass has developed a deep, golden colour, and there are a few small dents from when it was dropped during a house move decades ago.

We light it every evening during festivals and on special occasions, such as birthdays and weddings. Lighting the lamp is a way of welcoming good fortune into the home, so it has a spiritual meaning as well.

The reason it's so important to my family, though, is that it connects the generations. When I see it lit, I think of my grandmother, who taught me how to clean it properly with tamarind, and of all the family celebrations it has been part of. It's not worth much money, but we would never sell it. One day, I hope it will be passed on to my own children.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Why do people keep old things?**

> Often for sentimental reasons — objects remind us of people and events that are important to us. Some people also keep old things because they're well made, or because they think they might become valuable.

**Do young people value old objects?**

> Some do, especially as vintage fashion and furniture have become popular. But many young people prefer new, convenient products and don't have space to keep things in small apartments.

**Should old buildings be preserved?**

> I think historically significant buildings should be, because they're part of a city's identity. However, it's not realistic to preserve every old building, especially in cities that need new housing.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
