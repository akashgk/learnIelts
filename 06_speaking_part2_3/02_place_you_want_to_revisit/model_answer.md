# Model answer — A Place You Want to Revisit

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

The place I'd love to revisit is Munnar, a hill station in the Western Ghats mountains in Kerala, in southern India. I went there about three years ago with a group of friends from college, just after our final exams.

It's about four hours' drive from where I live, and the journey itself is part of the experience, because the road winds up through forests and past waterfalls. As you climb, the temperature drops noticeably, which is a real relief if you're used to the heat of the coast.

We stayed there for three days. The landscape is dominated by tea plantations — endless green hills covered in neatly trimmed tea bushes — and we spent one morning visiting a tea factory to see how the leaves are processed. We also went trekking to a viewpoint where you could see the clouds below you, which was absolutely breathtaking. In the evenings we just sat around a fire, chatting and drinking fresh tea.

The reason I'd like to go back is that the trip was far too short. We were rushing from one sight to the next, and I'd love to go back and just slow down — maybe stay in a small homestay, walk without any particular plan and enjoy the silence. I'd also really like to take my parents, because they've never been, and I think they'd find it as peaceful as I did. Places like that are a perfect escape from the noise and stress of city life.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Why do some people prefer to visit the same place again and again?**

> I think it's partly about comfort and familiarity. When you go back to a place you know, there's no stress about planning, and you can relax immediately. Some people also enjoy noticing how a place changes over time, or they've built relationships with local people there.

**What are the effects of tourism on natural areas?**

> It can be a double-edged sword. On the one hand, tourism brings money that can be used for conservation and gives local people jobs. On the other hand, too many visitors can lead to litter, erosion and pressure on water supplies, so careful management is essential.

**Should the number of tourists visiting some places be limited?**

> Yes, in fragile places I think limits are sensible. Some national parks already require permits, and while it means fewer people can visit, it protects the environment for future generations, which is more important in the long run.

## Useful phrases

- *the road winds up through …*
- *absolutely breathtaking*
- *a perfect escape from …*
- *a double-edged sword*
- *in the long run*

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
