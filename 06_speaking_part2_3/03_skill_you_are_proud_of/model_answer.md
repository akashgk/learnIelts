# Model answer — A Skill You Are Proud Of

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

A skill I'm genuinely proud of is swimming. That might sound surprising, because most people learn to swim as children, but I didn't learn until I was twenty-two.

When I was young, I had a frightening experience in a river, and after that I avoided water completely. As I got older, I became quite embarrassed about it — whenever friends went to the beach, I'd make excuses or just sit on the sand.

Eventually, I decided enough was enough and signed up for an adult beginners' course at a local pool. There were about six of us in the class, all adults, which made it much less embarrassing. The lessons were twice a week for three months.

Honestly, the technical side wasn't the hardest part — it was the fear. For the first few weeks, I couldn't even put my face in the water without panicking. The instructor was incredibly patient. She broke everything down into tiny steps: first just breathing out under water, then floating while holding the side, and so on. Slowly my confidence grew.

Now I can swim a kilometre without stopping, and I go to the pool at least once a week. The reason I'm so proud of it is that it wasn't really about swimming — it was about facing something I'd been afraid of for most of my life. It showed me that it's never too late to learn something, and that fear can be overcome if you take things one small step at a time.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**What skills do you think children should learn at school apart from academic subjects?**

> Practical life skills, definitely — things like managing money, cooking and basic first aid. I'd also add communication skills, such as giving presentations and working in teams, because these are essential in almost every job.

**Is it easier to learn new skills as a child or as an adult?**

> In some ways children have the advantage, because their brains are very adaptable and they're less self-conscious about making mistakes. But adults often have more motivation and discipline, and they can choose what they want to learn, so I think adults shouldn't underestimate themselves.

**Do you think technology has changed the way people learn skills?**

> Absolutely. You can now learn almost anything from online videos, from playing the guitar to fixing a bicycle. The downside is that without a teacher, it's easy to develop bad habits, and many people start online courses but never finish them.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
