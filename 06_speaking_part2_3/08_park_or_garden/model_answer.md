# Model answer — A Park or Garden You Like

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

The place I'd like to describe is Lalbagh, a large botanical garden in the south of Bangalore, which I visited regularly when I lived there for work.

It's a really old garden — it was originally laid out in the eighteenth century — and it covers a huge area. There are wide lawns, a lake, and some enormous ancient trees, some of which are said to be hundreds of years old. The most famous feature is a glasshouse, a beautiful structure made of iron and glass, which hosts flower shows twice a year.

What I find interesting is how many different people use it. Early in the morning it's full of joggers and walkers, and groups of elderly people doing yoga or laughing exercises. Later in the day you see families having picnics, students sitting under the trees with their books, and lots of tourists taking photos. During the flower shows it gets extremely crowded, but the displays are spectacular.

I liked going there mainly because it was an escape from the city. Bangalore is busy and the traffic is terrible, but once you're inside the garden, the noise fades and the air feels noticeably fresher. I used to go on Sunday mornings to walk around the lake and just clear my head before the week started.

Even though I've moved away now, whenever I go back to Bangalore I try to visit, because it brings back lots of good memories from that time in my life.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Why are parks important in cities?**

> They provide space for exercise and relaxation, which is important for both physical and mental health. Trees also help to cool cities and reduce air pollution, and parks give people from different backgrounds a place to meet.

**Should city land be used for parks or for housing?**

> That's a difficult balance, especially in cities with housing shortages. But I'd argue that green space shouldn't be sacrificed entirely, because without it cities become unhealthy and unpleasant places to live. Building higher-density housing can free up space for parks.

**How do people's leisure activities in parks differ between generations?**

> Older people tend to use parks for walking, gentle exercise and socialising, while young people might come for sports, picnics or just to hang out with friends. Families with children often focus on playgrounds.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
