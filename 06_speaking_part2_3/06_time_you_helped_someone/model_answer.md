# Model answer — A Time You Helped Someone

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

I'd like to talk about a time last winter when I helped my neighbour, Mr Thomas, who's in his late seventies and lives alone in the flat below mine.

One evening I heard a noise from the stairwell and found him sitting on the steps — he'd slipped and couldn't stand up. I called an ambulance and went with him to the hospital, where it turned out he'd broken his leg. His children live abroad, so he didn't really have anyone nearby to help.

Over the next six weeks, while he was in a cast, I did whatever I could. I picked up his groceries and medicine, took his rubbish out, and usually dropped in for twenty minutes or so after work just to check he was all right. Sometimes I'd help him with things on his phone, like video-calling his daughter.

At first I thought of it as a duty — it just seemed like the right thing to do. But very quickly, it became something I looked forward to. Mr Thomas used to be a ship's engineer and he has amazing stories about travelling around the world, so our conversations were genuinely interesting.

In terms of how I felt, it was really rewarding. It made me realise how easy it is to live next to people without knowing anything about them. We're good friends now — I still visit him every week, and he insists on cooking for me, so I think I've gained as much as he has.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Why do some people not help others?**

> Often it's not because they don't care, but because they're busy or don't notice that someone needs help. In big cities, people can also be wary of strangers, or assume that someone else will step in.

**Should schools teach children to help others?**

> Yes, I think so. Schools could organise volunteering projects or encourage older students to mentor younger ones. Learning empathy early makes it more likely that people will help others as adults.

**Do you think people help each other less than in the past?**

> In some ways, yes, because communities are less close-knit and people move more often. But technology has also created new ways to help — like online fundraising — so I'd say help has changed form rather than disappeared.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
