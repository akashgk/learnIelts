# Model answer — A Time You Were Very Busy

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

The busiest period I can remember was during the final semester of my degree, about three years ago.

Several things happened at the same time. I had to finish my final-year project, which was a large piece of software plus a long written report. At the same time I had a part-time job in a café, working three evenings a week, which I couldn't give up because I needed the money. And to make things even more complicated, my landlord decided to sell the flat, so I had to find a new place and move in the middle of everything.

To survive, I had to become much more organised than usual. Every Sunday evening I wrote a detailed plan for the week, breaking my project into small tasks. I started waking up at six to work on my report before university, because I found I could concentrate best early in the morning. I also had to learn to say no — I missed quite a few social events, which was hard.

As for how I felt, it was definitely stressful. There were a couple of nights when I barely slept, and I remember feeling exhausted most of the time. But looking back, I'm quite proud of how I handled it. I submitted the project on time, got a good grade, and managed to move without losing my job.

It also taught me a lot about time management, and I still use the Sunday planning habit today.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Do you think people today are busier than in the past?**

> In many ways, yes. Technology means people can work at any time and are constantly connected, so there's less clear separation between work and rest. People also try to fit more activities into their lives, including hobbies, exercise and socialising.

**What are the effects of being too busy?**

> Constant busyness can lead to stress, poor sleep and eventually burnout. It can also harm relationships, because people don't have enough time for their families and friends.

**How can people achieve a better work–life balance?**

> Setting clear boundaries helps — for example, not checking work emails in the evening. Employers also have a role, by offering flexible hours and discouraging excessive overtime.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
