# Model answer — A Memorable Journey

## Part 2 · Model talk (about 2 minutes)

I'd like to describe a train journey I took from Chennai to Delhi when I was nineteen. I was going to start an internship, and it was the first time I had travelled such a long distance on my own.

The journey took about thirty-three hours, so it involved two nights on the train. I was travelling in a sleeper carriage, where the seats convert into bunks at night. I'll admit I was quite nervous at the start, because I didn't know anyone and I'd heard stories about long-distance trains being uncomfortable.

But it turned out to be one of the most memorable experiences of my life. The people in my compartment were a mixture of families, students and a retired couple, and within a few hours we were all chatting. The elderly couple insisted on sharing their home-made food with me, and in the evening some of the students taught me a card game that we played until late.

I also remember just watching the landscape change through the window — from green coconut palms in the south to dry plains and then fields of wheat as we went north. It made me realise how huge and diverse my country is.

The reason it was so memorable is that it was a kind of turning point. I set off feeling anxious and unsure of myself, and I arrived feeling much more independent and confident. It also taught me that strangers are often far kinder than we expect.

## Part 3 · Model answers

**Do you think people will travel more or less in the future?**

> Probably more, as incomes rise in many countries and travel becomes more accessible. However, concerns about climate change might encourage people to fly less and choose closer destinations or slower options like trains.

**What are the advantages of travelling by train rather than by plane?**

> Trains are generally better for the environment, and they take you from city centre to city centre, so you avoid long journeys to airports. You can also move around, work and enjoy the scenery, which makes the journey part of the experience.

**Why do some people enjoy travelling alone?**

> Travelling alone gives you complete freedom to decide where to go and when. It also pushes you out of your comfort zone, so you tend to meet more local people and learn more about yourself.

## How to do Part 2 well
- **1 minute prep:** write 4–6 keywords, one per bullet point. Don't write sentences.
- **Speak for the full 2 minutes.** Cover every bullet, but spend longest on the last one ("explain why…") — it's where you show range.
- **Structure:** introduce → tell the story with past tenses → reflect on why it matters.
- **Signposting:** *I'd like to talk about…; What I remember most is…; The reason… is that…; Looking back…*
- **Part 3** is more abstract: give an answer, a reason, an example, and if possible a contrasting point (*That said…, On the other hand…*).
