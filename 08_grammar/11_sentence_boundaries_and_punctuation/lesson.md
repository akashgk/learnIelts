# Grammar — Sentence Boundaries & Punctuation

*Avoid comma splices and fragments; use commas, semicolons and colons correctly.*

## The two most common errors
1. **Comma splice** — two complete sentences joined only by a comma.
   - ❌ *Pollution is rising, governments must act.*
   - ✅ *Pollution is rising, **so** governments must act.* / *Pollution is rising**;** governments must act.* / *Pollution is rising**.** Governments must act.*
2. **Fragment** — an incomplete sentence.
   - ❌ *Because many people live alone.*
   - ✅ *Loneliness is increasing because many people live alone.*

## Commas
- After introductory linkers/clauses: ***However,** … · **Although prices rose,** demand stayed high.*
- Around non-defining relative clauses: *Paris, **which** is the capital, …*
- **No** comma before a defining relative clause or *that*-clause: *People who exercise… · He said that…*

## Semicolons and colons
- **;** joins two closely related sentences, or comes before *however/therefore* mid-sentence: *Costs rose; however, sales grew.*
- **:** introduces a list or explanation: *There are two causes: poverty and poor education.*
