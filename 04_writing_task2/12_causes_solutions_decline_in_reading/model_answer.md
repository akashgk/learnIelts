# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Surveys in many countries suggest that fewer children and teenagers now read books in their free time. This essay will explore the reasons for this decline and propose some ways in which young people could be encouraged to rediscover reading.

The most significant cause is competition from digital entertainment. Smartphones, video platforms and online games offer instant, highly stimulating rewards, and they are deliberately designed to capture users' attention for as long as possible. Compared with these, a book, which requires sustained concentration before it becomes absorbing, can seem slow and demanding. A second reason is that reading is increasingly associated with schoolwork. When books are encountered mainly as set texts to be analysed for examinations, many students come to see reading as a chore rather than a pleasure. Finally, children are strongly influenced by the adults around them, and in households where parents rarely read, young people have few role models.

Several measures could help to reverse this trend. Schools could set aside time each day for independent reading in which students choose any book they like — including comics, graphic novels or non-fiction — without being tested on it, so that reading is linked with enjoyment rather than assessment. Public libraries also have an important role to play: by offering reading clubs, author visits and attractive spaces for teenagers, they can make reading a social activity. At home, parents can read aloud to young children and model the habit themselves by reading in front of them. Technology need not be the enemy either; e-readers and audiobooks can introduce stories to young people who are more comfortable with screens.

In conclusion, the decline in reading for pleasure stems largely from digital distractions, the association of books with study and a lack of role models. Giving young people free choice, making reading sociable and setting a good example at home could all help to revive the habit.
