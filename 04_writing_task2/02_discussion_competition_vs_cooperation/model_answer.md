# Model essay (Band 8–9)

There is ongoing debate about whether schools should encourage pupils to compete with one another or to work together. Although competition has some value, I believe that cooperation is the more important skill for children to develop.

Those who favour a competitive approach argue that it prepares children for adult life. University places, jobs and promotions are all allocated competitively, so pupils who learn early to strive to outperform their peers may be better equipped to succeed later. Competition can also be highly motivating: a spelling contest or a sports tournament often inspires children to practise far harder than they would otherwise. In addition, it teaches them to cope with failure, since not everyone can win.

On the other hand, advocates of cooperation point out that most achievements in the modern world are collective rather than individual. Scientific research, business projects and public services all depend on people with different skills sharing ideas and dividing responsibilities. Children who regularly work in groups learn to listen, negotiate and resolve disagreements — abilities that employers consistently rank among the most valuable. Moreover, an excessively competitive environment can damage the self-esteem of weaker pupils, who may come to see themselves as permanent 'losers' and disengage from learning altogether.

In my view, the second argument is more persuasive. While a limited amount of friendly competition can make lessons enjoyable, the ability to collaborate is more fundamental, both to individual success and to the functioning of society. Indeed, even competitive fields such as professional sport reward teamwork. Schools should therefore prioritise cooperative learning, using competition only occasionally and in ways that do not humiliate those who lose.

In conclusion, while both approaches have merit, teaching children to cooperate should take precedence, as it better reflects the demands of modern life and supports the well-being of all pupils.

*(301 words)*

---

## Essay plan

| Paragraph | Content |
|---|---|
| Intro | Paraphrase both views + **my opinion: cooperation is more important** |
| Body 1 | View A (competition): prepares for adult life, motivation, coping with failure |
| Body 2 | View B (cooperation): collective achievement, employability, self-esteem |
| Body 3 | My opinion with reasons (could be merged into Body 2 for a 4-paragraph essay) |
| Conclusion | Summarise and restate |

## Key language

- **Presenting views:** *Those who favour…argue that…; Advocates of… point out that…; It is often claimed that…*
- **Evaluating:** *the second argument is more persuasive; has some merit; should take precedence*
- **Vocabulary:** *outperform their peers, highly motivating, collaborate, negotiate, resolve disagreements, self-esteem, disengage, cooperative learning*

## Common mistake
❌ Writing "In my opinion" in the body paragraph that describes **other people's** view. Keep your opinion clearly separate so the examiner can see you have discussed both views objectively.
