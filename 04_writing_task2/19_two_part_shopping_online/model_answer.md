# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Over the past two decades, a growing share of retail spending has moved from physical shops to the internet. This essay will discuss the reasons for this change and its consequences for the centres of towns and cities.

The main attraction of online shopping is convenience. Consumers can browse and buy products at any hour without travelling, queuing or carrying heavy bags, and fast home delivery has made waiting times increasingly short. Online retailers also offer a far wider choice than any single shop, and price-comparison websites allow customers to find the cheapest option within seconds. Furthermore, smartphones have made purchasing almost effortless, while customer reviews give shoppers confidence about products they cannot see or touch in person.

This shift has had serious consequences for town centres. As footfall declines, many shops — particularly chain stores and independent retailers with high rents — have been forced to close, leaving empty units that make high streets look neglected. This can create a vicious circle: fewer shops attract fewer visitors, which leads to further closures and job losses. Local councils also lose income from business taxes, reducing the money available for maintaining public spaces.

However, the effects are not entirely negative. Some town centres are adapting by becoming places for experiences rather than purchases, with cafés, restaurants, gyms and cultural venues replacing traditional shops. Empty commercial buildings can also be converted into housing, bringing residents back into city centres and supporting local businesses.

In conclusion, online shopping is booming because of its convenience, choice and low prices. Although this has harmed many traditional high streets, it also creates an opportunity to reinvent town centres as places to live and socialise rather than simply to shop.

## Key moves
- A **"However, the effects are not entirely negative"** paragraph adds nuance to Q2 without contradicting the main point.
- **Cause → effect chain:** *fewer shops attract fewer visitors, which leads to further closures*.
- **Vocabulary:** *footfall, high streets, empty units, vicious circle, business taxes, reinvent, experiences rather than purchases*.
