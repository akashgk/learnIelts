# Model essay (Band 8–9)

In many countries, the rising cost of higher education has prompted calls for university tuition to be made free for everyone. While I accept that no capable student should be excluded from university for financial reasons, I disagree that tuition should be free for all, as this would be both unfair and economically unsustainable.

The strongest argument for free university education is that it promotes equality of opportunity. When students must pay high fees, those from low-income families are often deterred from applying, even if they are highly talented, because they fear accumulating debt. This represents a loss not only for the individuals concerned but also for society, which is deprived of the doctors, engineers and teachers they might have become. Countries such as Germany, which abolished tuition fees at public universities, are often cited as evidence that free education can widen access.

However, making university free for every student, irrespective of their family's wealth, has serious drawbacks. Since the cost must be covered by taxation, workers who never attended university — many of whom earn modest wages — would effectively subsidise the education of graduates who will go on to earn significantly more over their lifetimes. Furthermore, universally free tuition places a heavy burden on public finances, which can lead to overcrowded classes and underfunded institutions, ultimately lowering the quality of education for everyone.

A fairer approach, in my view, would be a targeted system. Students from disadvantaged backgrounds could receive full grants, while others pay fees through income-contingent loans that are repaid only once graduates earn above a certain threshold. Such a system protects access for the poorest without asking the general public to fund the education of the wealthy.

In conclusion, although the goal of free higher education is admirable, I believe that financial support should be directed at those who genuinely need it rather than extended to all students.

*(311 words)*

## Essay plan (5 minutes before writing)

| Paragraph | Content |
|---|---|
| Introduction | Paraphrase + **position**: partly agree — support for poor students, but not free for all |
| Body 1 | Concession: the case FOR free education (equality of opportunity) + example (Germany) |
| Body 2 | Main reason AGAINST: unfair to non-graduate taxpayers + pressure on public finances |
| Body 3 | Alternative: targeted grants + income-contingent loans |
| Conclusion | Restate position in new words |

## Why it scores highly

- **Task Response:** position is clear in the introduction and conclusion, and the body paragraphs support it. The concession paragraph shows balance without contradicting the position.
- **Coherence:** each paragraph has a clear topic sentence. Linking is natural: *However, Furthermore, Since, Such a system*.
- **Lexical Resource:** *deterred, accumulating debt, deprived of, irrespective of, subsidise, income-contingent, threshold, targeted system*.
- **Grammar:** relative clauses (*which abolished…, many of whom…*), conditional ideas, passive (*are often cited*), participle phrases.

## Topic vocabulary — education & finance
*tuition fees · student loans · means-tested grants · widen access / participation · social mobility · the taxpayer · public funding · graduate premium · underfunded · a level playing field*
