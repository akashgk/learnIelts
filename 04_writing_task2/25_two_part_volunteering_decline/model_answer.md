# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Voluntary organisations, from charities to sports clubs, depend on people giving their time without payment. In several countries, however, the number of regular volunteers has declined. This essay will examine the reasons for this trend and suggest ways to reverse it.

The most important reason is lack of time. Many adults now work longer or less predictable hours, and in many families both parents are employed, leaving little time for activities outside work and childcare. The rise of temporary and shift work also makes it difficult for people to commit to volunteering at the same time each week. A second factor is the weakening of community ties. People move home more frequently than in the past and may not feel connected to local organisations, while traditional routes into volunteering, such as religious groups and social clubs, have lost members. Finally, some people are discouraged by bureaucracy, such as long application forms and training requirements.

Several steps could encourage more people to volunteer. Organisations could offer more flexible opportunities, such as one-off events, short projects or tasks that can be done online, which suit people with busy or irregular schedules. Employers could also play a role by giving staff a few paid days each year for volunteering, a policy that many companies have found improves morale. Schools and universities could introduce students to volunteering early, helping them to develop a lifelong habit. Finally, governments and charities should simplify application processes where possible and publicly recognise the contribution of volunteers, so that people feel their time is valued.

In conclusion, volunteering has declined mainly because of time pressures, weaker communities and bureaucratic barriers. Offering flexible opportunities, involving employers and schools, and reducing paperwork could help to attract a new generation of volunteers.

*(290 words)*

---

## Key moves
- Each solution answers a cause: time pressure → flexible, one-off and online roles; weak ties → schools and employers; bureaucracy → simpler processes.
- **Vocabulary:** *commit to, shift work, community ties, bureaucracy, one-off, morale, lifelong habit, recognise the contribution*.
