# Model essay (Band 8–9)

In many workplaces, long hours are seen as a sign of dedication and ambition. Others, however, argue that shorter working weeks lead to better results and greater well-being. In my opinion, while hard work is important, working excessively long hours is usually counterproductive.

Those who believe long hours are necessary point to the careers of successful entrepreneurs, doctors and lawyers, many of whom have worked extremely long weeks, particularly at the start of their careers. In competitive fields, putting in extra time can help people gain experience faster, build a reputation and stand out from their colleagues. Some argue that certain goals, such as launching a business, simply cannot be achieved in a standard working week.

On the other hand, many people contend that working fewer hours leads to higher productivity. Concentration declines when people are tired, so the extra hours worked at the end of a long day often produce little of value and may even lead to costly mistakes. Several companies that have trialled four-day weeks have reported that output remained stable or improved, while staff were less stressed and less likely to take sick leave. Furthermore, people with time to rest, exercise and see their families tend to be healthier and more motivated.

I find the second view more convincing. Short periods of intense effort may be necessary at times, but sustained overwork leads to exhaustion, poorer decisions and, eventually, burnout. Success depends more on how effectively people use their time than on how much of it they spend at work.

In conclusion, although long hours can sometimes help people advance, I believe a reasonable working week produces better results for both employees and employers in the long term.

## Key moves
- **Concession inside the opinion:** *Short periods of intense effort may be necessary at times, but…*
- **Evidence without invented numbers:** *Several companies … have reported that…*
- **Vocabulary:** *dedication, counterproductive, stand out, contend, output, sick leave, sustained overwork, burnout*.
