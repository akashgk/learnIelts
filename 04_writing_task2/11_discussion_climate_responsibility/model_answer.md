# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Environmental problems such as climate change and plastic pollution raise the question of who is responsible for solving them. While some argue that only governments and corporations have the power to make a difference, others insist that individuals must play their part. In my view, both are necessary, but the actions of governments and businesses are decisive.

Those who emphasise institutional responsibility point out that the scale of environmental damage is far beyond what any individual can address. A small number of industries, including energy production, heavy manufacturing and agriculture, account for the majority of greenhouse gas emissions, and only governments can regulate them through laws, taxes and investment in clean technology. From this perspective, asking individuals to recycle or take shorter showers may even be counterproductive, because it distracts attention from the systemic changes that are really needed.

On the other hand, advocates of individual responsibility argue that collective change is made up of millions of personal choices. Consumers who buy fewer disposable products, eat less meat or choose public transport reduce demand for polluting goods and services. Equally importantly, individuals are also voters and employees. When enough citizens show that they care about the environment, politicians are more likely to introduce ambitious policies and companies are more likely to adopt sustainable practices in order to protect their reputation.

I believe that these two positions are complementary rather than opposed. Governments and large companies have the greatest capacity to reduce emissions, so they must lead. However, they rarely act without public pressure, and that pressure comes from individuals who change their own behaviour and demand change from those in power.

In conclusion, while individual actions alone cannot solve environmental problems, they remain important, both directly and because they drive the larger-scale changes that only institutions can deliver.

*(298 words)*

---

## Key moves
- **Complementary rather than opposed** — a strong way to link both views while still taking a position ("institutions are decisive").
- The insight that individuals are also **voters and employees** develops the argument beyond the obvious (recycling).
- **Vocabulary:** *institutional, systemic, counterproductive, regulate, disposable, sustainable practices, public pressure, capacity, drive change*.
