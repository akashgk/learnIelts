# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Famous athletes, musicians and online personalities attract millions of young followers, many of whom regard them as role models. This essay will consider why celebrities are so influential among young people and argue that, while some of this influence is beneficial, it is on balance more negative than positive.

There are several reasons for the power of celebrities. Firstly, social media has made them seem more accessible than ever. Influencers share apparently intimate details of their daily lives, creating a sense of personal connection that makes followers trust their opinions and recommendations. Secondly, adolescence is a stage of life in which people are actively forming their identities, so they naturally look for models of who they might become. Celebrities, who appear confident, attractive and successful, are an obvious source of inspiration. Finally, the sheer amount of time young people spend online means they are exposed to celebrity content far more often than to other potential role models, such as teachers or community leaders.

Admittedly, this influence can be positive. Sports stars can demonstrate the value of discipline and hard work, and some public figures use their platforms to raise awareness of important causes, such as mental health or climate change, encouraging young people to get involved.

However, I believe the negative effects are more significant. Much celebrity content promotes unrealistic standards of appearance and wealth, often enhanced by filters and editing, which can damage young people's self-esteem and body image. Moreover, many influencers earn money by advertising products, and young followers may not recognise that recommendations are paid promotions. As a result, celebrity culture can encourage materialism and the belief that fame itself is a worthwhile goal.

In conclusion, celebrities influence young people because of social media, the search for identity and constant exposure. Although some set a good example, I consider their overall influence to be more harmful than helpful.

*(311 words)*

## Key moves
- Q1 (**why**) gets three reasons with clear sequencing; Q2 (**positive or negative**) gets a concession paragraph then the main argument.
- **Admittedly, … However, I believe…** — a natural concession pattern.
- **Vocabulary:** *look up to, role models, accessible, intimate details, adolescence, forming identities, platforms, raise awareness, unrealistic standards, self-esteem, paid promotions, materialism*.
