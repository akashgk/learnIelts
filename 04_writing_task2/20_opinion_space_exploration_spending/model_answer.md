# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Governments spend billions of dollars each year on space programmes, and critics argue that this money should instead be used to tackle poverty, disease and climate change. Although I understand this view, I disagree, because space exploration delivers practical benefits on Earth and represents only a small share of public spending.

Those who oppose space spending make a powerful moral argument. When millions of people lack clean water, adequate healthcare or education, sending robots to Mars can appear to be a luxury. From this perspective, every dollar spent on rockets is a dollar not spent on hospitals or schools.

However, this argument overlooks how much space research contributes to solving earthly problems. Satellites, which exist only because of space programmes, are essential for weather forecasting, disaster warnings, navigation and global communications. Crucially, they also provide much of the data scientists use to monitor climate change, deforestation and crop health. In addition, technologies first developed for space missions have found widespread applications in medicine, materials science and computing. Space exploration therefore often complements, rather than competes with, efforts to improve life on Earth.

Moreover, the sums involved are relatively modest. Even in countries with large space agencies, the space budget is a small fraction of total government spending, so cutting it would not transform funding for health or education. Finally, space exploration inspires young people to study science and engineering, building the skilled workforce that every modern economy needs.

In conclusion, while the problems facing humanity on Earth are urgent, I believe that abandoning space exploration would be short-sighted. Its scientific, technological and educational benefits contribute directly to solving those very problems.

*(271 words)*

## Key moves
- **Fair concession paragraph** followed by *However, this argument overlooks…* — a strong rebuttal pattern.
- The essay **turns the opponent's point around**: space research *helps* solve earthly problems (climate monitoring).
- **Vocabulary:** *luxury, overlooks, disaster warnings, complements rather than competes with, a small fraction of, short-sighted, skilled workforce*.
