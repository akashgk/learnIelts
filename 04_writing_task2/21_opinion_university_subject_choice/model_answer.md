# Model essay (Band 8–9)

With rising tuition costs and fierce competition for jobs, some argue that universities should focus exclusively on subjects with obvious practical value, such as science and technology. While I accept that these fields are vital, I strongly disagree that universities should offer nothing else.

Admittedly, there are understandable reasons for prioritising science and technology. Economies increasingly depend on engineers, data scientists and medical researchers, and shortages in these areas can slow growth and innovation. Graduates in such subjects also tend to find well-paid employment more easily, which matters to students who have taken on large loans.

However, defining 'useful' so narrowly is a serious mistake. Subjects such as history, philosophy, languages and literature develop skills that employers consistently value: the ability to analyse complex information, construct an argument and communicate clearly. Moreover, many of the most pressing challenges facing society are not purely technical. Questions about the ethical use of artificial intelligence, for instance, require an understanding of philosophy, law and human behaviour as much as computer science. A society of excellent technicians who lack these perspectives would be poorly equipped to make wise decisions.

There is also the problem of prediction. Nobody can know with certainty which skills will be in demand in twenty years' time; many of today's jobs did not exist a generation ago. Restricting universities to subjects that appear useful now could leave societies unprepared for future changes. Finally, universities have a cultural role in preserving and developing knowledge for its own sake, a function that cannot be measured in salaries alone.

In conclusion, although science and technology deserve strong support, universities should continue to offer a broad range of subjects, since the skills and perspectives developed in the humanities are equally essential.

*(287 words)*

## Key moves
- **Challenging a definition** (*defining 'useful' so narrowly*) is a powerful Band 8 technique.
- The **AI ethics example** connects the two sides cleverly.
- **Vocabulary:** *exclusively, pressing challenges, ethical, perspectives, in demand, for its own sake*.
