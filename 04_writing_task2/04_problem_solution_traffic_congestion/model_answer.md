# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Gridlocked roads have become a familiar feature of life in many of the world's largest cities, wasting time, damaging health and harming economic productivity. This essay will examine the principal causes of this problem and suggest some practical measures to address it.

The root cause of congestion is that the number of vehicles has grown far faster than the capacity of urban roads. As incomes rise, particularly in rapidly developing economies, car ownership becomes both affordable and a symbol of status, so millions of new drivers join the roads each year. This problem is compounded by inadequate public transport. Where buses are slow and unreliable, or where metro systems do not reach the suburbs, residents have little choice but to drive. Urban sprawl makes the situation worse: when homes are built far from workplaces, schools and shops, even short daily errands require a car journey.

Several measures could help to tackle these causes. First, governments should invest heavily in fast, frequent and affordable public transport, such as dedicated bus lanes and extended rail networks, so that leaving the car at home becomes the more convenient option. Second, the true cost of driving should be reflected in its price. Congestion charges, like those introduced in London and Stockholm, have been shown to reduce traffic in city centres while generating revenue that can be reinvested in transport. Finally, in the longer term, planners should design compact neighbourhoods in which housing, employment and services are located close together, enabling people to walk or cycle for many of their daily needs.

In conclusion, traffic congestion results mainly from rising car ownership, poor public transport and sprawling urban development. Addressing it will require a combination of better alternatives to driving, pricing mechanisms that discourage unnecessary car use, and smarter urban planning.

*(296 words)*

## Essay plan

| Cause | → Matching solution |
|---|---|
| More cars (rising incomes, status) | Congestion charge (price signal) |
| Poor public transport | Invest in bus lanes, rail |
| Urban sprawl | Compact, mixed-use neighbourhoods |

## Useful structures
- **Cause → effect:** *This problem is compounded by…; …makes the situation worse; As a result,…; …results mainly from…*
- **Solutions:** *Governments should invest in…; …could help to tackle…; In the longer term, planners should…*
- **Evidence without statistics:** *Congestion charges, like those introduced in London and Stockholm, have been shown to…* — real-world examples strengthen Task Response, but never invent precise figures.
