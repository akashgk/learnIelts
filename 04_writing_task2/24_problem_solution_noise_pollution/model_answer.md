# Model essay (Band 8–9)

As cities grow larger and busier, many residents complain that they can rarely escape noise. This is more than an inconvenience: prolonged exposure to loud noise has been linked to sleep disturbance, stress and heart disease. This essay will discuss the main causes of urban noise and suggest ways to reduce it.

The most significant source of noise in most cities is road traffic. The steady rise in the number of cars, motorcycles and delivery vehicles has filled streets with engine noise, horns and the sound of tyres on the road surface. Construction is another major contributor, as growing cities are constantly building new homes, offices and infrastructure, often with heavy machinery operating from early in the morning. Finally, the increasing density of urban living means that people live closer to bars, restaurants, entertainment venues and each other, so noise from neighbours and nightlife affects more people than before.

Several measures could reduce this problem. To tackle traffic noise, authorities can lower speed limits, which significantly reduces tyre and engine noise, and encourage the use of quieter electric vehicles and public transport. Roads can be resurfaced with low-noise asphalt, and barriers or rows of trees can be placed alongside major roads. Construction noise can be controlled by strict rules on working hours and by requiring quieter equipment. In addition, building regulations should require better sound insulation, such as double-glazed windows, in new homes, especially those near busy roads or airports. Lastly, careful urban planning can keep noisy activities, such as nightlife districts, separate from quiet residential areas.

In conclusion, urban noise is caused mainly by traffic, construction and the density of city life. A combination of traffic management, building regulations and thoughtful planning could make cities considerably quieter and healthier places to live.

## Key moves
- The introduction briefly states **why the problem matters** (health effects) — this strengthens Task Response.
- Solutions are **grouped by cause** (traffic → speed limits, surfaces; construction → hours, equipment; density → planning).
- **Vocabulary:** *prolonged exposure, sleep disturbance, contributor, density, resurfaced, low-noise asphalt, sound insulation, double-glazed*.
