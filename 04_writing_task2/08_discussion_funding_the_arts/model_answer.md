# Model essay (Band 8–9)

How public money should be divided between cultural activities and essential services is a recurring question for governments. While some argue that funding the arts is a luxury, I believe that a modest but secure level of public support for culture is fully justified, even alongside pressing needs in health and education.

Those who oppose arts funding argue that governments have a duty to prioritise services on which lives and livelihoods depend. When hospitals have long waiting lists and schools lack teachers, spending money on an opera house or a new art gallery may seem difficult to defend. Moreover, they claim that successful artistic activities can attract private sponsors and paying audiences, so state support is unnecessary, and that it disproportionately benefits wealthier, better-educated citizens who are most likely to attend.

On the other hand, supporters of arts funding contend that culture brings both economic and social returns. Museums, festivals and theatres draw tourists, create jobs and help to regenerate run-down urban areas. Public subsidies also allow galleries to offer free or reduced-price entry and support music and drama in schools, ensuring that cultural experiences are not restricted to those who can afford them. Beyond these practical benefits, the arts help to preserve a nation's heritage and give communities a shared sense of identity.

In my view, the choice is not as stark as it may appear. Arts budgets typically represent a very small fraction of public spending, so redirecting them would make little difference to healthcare or education, yet their removal could devastate cultural institutions. Governments should certainly prioritise essential services, but a small, stable investment in the arts offers excellent value.

In conclusion, although health and education must come first, I believe that continued public funding for the arts is a worthwhile investment in a society's economy, equality and identity.

*(302 words)*

---

## Key moves in this essay
- **Questioning the premise:** *"the choice is not as stark as it may appear"* — a sophisticated way to give a balanced but clear opinion.
- **Attributing views:** *Those who oppose… argue; they claim; supporters… contend*.
- **Vocabulary:** *pressing needs, livelihoods, private sponsors, disproportionately, regenerate, run-down, subsidies, heritage, devastate, worthwhile investment*.
