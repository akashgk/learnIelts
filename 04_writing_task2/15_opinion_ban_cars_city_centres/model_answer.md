# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Congestion and air pollution have led many to call for private cars to be excluded entirely from the centres of large cities. While I strongly support significant restrictions on car use in urban cores, I believe a complete ban would be impractical and, in some cases, unfair.

There are compelling reasons to reduce traffic in city centres. Vehicle exhaust is a major source of the fine particles and nitrogen dioxide that contribute to respiratory illness, and these pollutants are most concentrated in dense urban streets. Removing cars also frees up space that can be turned into pedestrian zones, cycle lanes and greenery, making centres safer and more pleasant. Cities that have pedestrianised their main streets, such as Copenhagen, have often seen footfall and retail spending increase rather than fall.

However, an outright ban would create genuine problems. People with disabilities or limited mobility may depend on private vehicles, and elderly residents could find themselves cut off from shops and services. Businesses also need deliveries, and tradespeople must carry tools and materials. Moreover, unless public transport is frequent, affordable and reliable, banning cars would simply make the city centre inaccessible to many residents of suburbs and surrounding towns.

A more effective approach would be to discourage unnecessary car journeys rather than forbid them altogether. Congestion charges, reduced parking and low-emission zones can sharply reduce traffic while still allowing essential access, with exemptions for disabled drivers and delivery vehicles. These measures should be introduced alongside substantial investment in public transport so that people have a realistic alternative.

In conclusion, although cars should play a much smaller role in city centres, I disagree with a total ban; carefully targeted restrictions combined with better public transport would achieve most of the benefits without the drawbacks.

*(291 words)*

## Key moves
- **Clear partial position** in the intro and conclusion: *strongly support restrictions … but a complete ban would be impractical*.
- Body 3 offers an **alternative** — a strong way to develop a partial-agreement essay.
- **Vocabulary:** *urban cores, respiratory illness, pedestrianised, footfall, limited mobility, outright ban, low-emission zones, exemptions*.
