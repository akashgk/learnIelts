# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Advances in digital technology have made it possible for a growing proportion of employees to carry out their jobs from home. This trend brings considerable benefits for both workers and employers, but it also creates significant challenges.

The most obvious advantage of remote work is the time and money it saves. Employees no longer need to spend hours commuting each week, which reduces travel costs and stress and gives them more time for their families or leisure activities. This flexibility can be particularly valuable for parents of young children and for people with disabilities, who may find a daily journey to an office difficult. Employers can also benefit, since they need less office space and can recruit talented staff from a much wider geographical area rather than only from those living within commuting distance.

However, working from home also has notable drawbacks. One of the most serious is social isolation. Offices provide informal contact with colleagues, and without it, many remote workers report feeling lonely and disconnected from their organisation. This lack of face-to-face interaction can also harm collaboration and the training of junior staff, who often learn by observing more experienced colleagues. A further problem is that the boundary between work and private life can become blurred. When the office is also the living room, employees may find it hard to switch off, leading to longer working hours and, in some cases, burnout.

In conclusion, working from home offers clear advantages in terms of flexibility, cost savings and access to talent, but these must be weighed against the risks of isolation, weaker teamwork and an unhealthy work–life balance. For many organisations, a hybrid arrangement combining home and office work may offer the best of both worlds.
