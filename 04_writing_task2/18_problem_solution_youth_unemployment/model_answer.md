# Model essay (Band 8–9)

Despite being more educated than any previous generation, many young people struggle to secure employment after completing their studies. This essay will examine the main causes of this problem and propose some measures that could help.

One major reason is the mismatch between what young people learn and what employers need. Many school and university courses focus on theoretical knowledge, while businesses increasingly look for practical and technical skills, as well as qualities such as teamwork and communication. A second cause is the 'experience trap': employers often prefer candidates with previous work experience, but young people cannot gain experience without first being given a job. Finally, economic factors play a role. During downturns, companies tend to freeze recruitment rather than dismiss existing staff, so new entrants to the labour market are hit hardest.

Several solutions could address these causes. Firstly, education systems should work more closely with employers to ensure that courses develop relevant skills. Expanding high-quality apprenticeships, which combine paid work with training, would allow young people to learn practical skills while earning. Secondly, governments could offer incentives, such as temporary tax reductions, to companies that hire and train young workers, helping them overcome the experience trap. Internship schemes should also be paid and properly structured so that they are accessible to everyone, not only those whose families can support them. Lastly, careers guidance in schools should be improved so that students make informed decisions about which subjects and qualifications lead to growing industries.

In conclusion, youth unemployment results from a mismatch of skills, a lack of experience and economic conditions. Closer cooperation between education and industry, incentives for employers and better careers advice could significantly improve young people's prospects.

*(281 words)*

## Key moves
- Coining a memorable label — *the 'experience trap'* — makes the argument clear and cohesive.
- Each solution maps onto a cause (skills mismatch → apprenticeships; experience trap → incentives/internships).
- **Vocabulary:** *secure employment, mismatch, freeze recruitment, labour market, apprenticeships, incentives, careers guidance, prospects*.
