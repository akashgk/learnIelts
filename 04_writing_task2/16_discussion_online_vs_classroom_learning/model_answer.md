# Model essay (Band 8–9)

The rapid growth of online education has prompted debate about whether it can match or even surpass traditional classroom teaching. While online courses offer important advantages, I believe that face-to-face learning remains more effective for most students, particularly when the two are combined.

Advocates of online learning emphasise its flexibility and accessibility. Students can study at any time and from any location, which makes education available to working adults, parents and people living far from universities. Recorded lectures can be paused and replayed, allowing learners to progress at their own pace, and online courses are often considerably cheaper because they require no buildings or travel. For highly motivated, independent learners, these benefits can be decisive.

On the other hand, supporters of classroom learning argue that education is a social process. In a physical classroom, teachers can immediately notice confusion and adjust their explanations, and students learn a great deal from discussion with their peers. Classes also provide structure and routine, which help many learners to stay motivated. This is reflected in the completion rates of many open online courses, which are often reported to be very low, suggesting that without the commitment of attending in person, many students give up.

In my view, the second argument is more persuasive for the majority of learners, especially younger students who are still developing the self-discipline required for independent study. However, rejecting online tools altogether would be a mistake. The most effective model is probably blended learning, in which online materials are used for content that students can study alone, freeing up classroom time for discussion, practice and feedback.

In conclusion, although online courses are valuable for their flexibility, face-to-face learning offers interaction and structure that most students need, and combining the two is likely to produce the best results.

*(297 words)*

## Key moves
- Evidence is **hedged**: *often reported to be very low* — never invent exact statistics.
- Opinion paragraph qualifies **who** the argument applies to (*especially younger students*) — precise positions score higher.
- **Vocabulary:** *surpass, flexibility, accessibility, at their own pace, peers, structure and routine, completion rates, self-discipline, blended learning*.
