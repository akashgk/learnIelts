# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The maps compare the village of Port Mira in 2000 with its present-day layout.

Overall, Port Mira has been transformed from a small fishing community into a tourist resort. Facilities connected with fishing and farming have been replaced by leisure and accommodation facilities, and access to the village has been improved, partly at the expense of the natural environment.

Along the coast, the area where fishing boats were once moored has been converted into a marina for yachts, and the old fish market has become a seafood restaurant. The beach to the east, which was previously undeveloped, is now lined with cafés.

Further inland, the fishermen's cottages in the west have been retained, but they are now used as holiday rentals. The most significant change has taken place on the eastern side, where the farmland has made way for a four-storey hotel with a swimming pool. In the south, the narrow road from the inland town has been widened, and a roundabout and a car park have been constructed. To make room for these developments, the forest in the south-east has been reduced to around half its original size.

*(190 words)*

---

## Examiner notes
- The overview states the **character** of the change (fishing → tourism) and the trade-off (access vs nature).
- *have been retained, but they are now used as* — useful when a building stays but its function changes.
- *made way for, at the expense of, to make room for* — natural phrases for replacement.
