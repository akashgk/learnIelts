# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The line graph compares the average cost of a home in Northport, Eastvale and Southbridge over a fourteen-year period starting in 2010.

Overall, property in Northport was the most expensive throughout and its price rose dramatically, while prices in Eastvale also increased considerably, particularly towards the end of the period. Southbridge, by contrast, saw almost no change.

In 2010, a home in Northport cost an average of $210,000, compared with $180,000 in Southbridge and $150,000 in Eastvale. Northport prices then climbed steeply, reaching $360,000 in 2019. After a slight dip to $340,000 in 2021, they resumed their upward trend, finishing at a peak of $395,000 — almost double the 2010 figure.

Eastvale's prices grew only gradually until 2019, when they stood at $180,000, but then accelerated sharply to $250,000 by 2024. As a result, Eastvale overtook Southbridge around 2017. Southbridge was the only city where prices remained broadly stable, fluctuating between $170,000 and $180,000 and ending the period at $178,000, slightly below their starting level.

## Examiner notes
- Three lines with **three different stories** (steep rise, late acceleration, stability) — the overview names all three.
- **Approximation:** *almost double, broadly stable, around 2017* — useful when lines cross between data points.
- **Dip / recovery:** *After a slight dip to…, they resumed their upward trend.*
- Notice the crossover: *Eastvale overtook Southbridge* — comparisons like this lift Task Achievement.
