# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The diagram illustrates the main stages in the life cycle of a butterfly, from egg to adult.

Overall, the cycle consists of four distinct stages — egg, caterpillar, chrysalis and adult butterfly — and repeats continuously, as each adult female produces eggs that begin the process again. The whole cycle takes roughly one to two months.

The cycle begins when an adult female lays between 100 and 300 eggs on a leaf. After three to seven days, these hatch into caterpillars, also known as larvae. During this stage, which lasts approximately two to three weeks, the caterpillar feeds on leaves and grows rapidly, shedding its skin four or five times as it increases in size.

Once fully grown, the caterpillar attaches itself to a plant stem and forms a chrysalis, or pupa. Over the following ten to fourteen days, its body undergoes a complete transformation inside this protective case. Finally, the adult butterfly emerges and spends a few hours drying its wings before it is able to fly. Adults live for between two and four weeks, during which time they mate, and the females lay eggs, completing the cycle.

## Examiner notes
- Natural cycles use mostly **active** verbs (*lays, hatch, feeds, emerges*). Use the passive only where it fits naturally.
- The **overview** states the number of stages, that it is a cycle, and the total duration.
- Include the **time data** — omitting numbers on a diagram is a common Task Achievement weakness.
- Technical terms + plain-English paraphrase: *caterpillars, also known as larvae; a chrysalis, or pupa*.
