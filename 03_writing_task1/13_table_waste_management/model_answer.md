# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The table compares the proportions of household waste that were recycled, composted, incinerated and sent to landfill in four countries in 2022.

Overall, there was a sharp contrast between Countries A and C, which disposed of very little waste in landfill sites, and Countries B and D, where landfill was the most common method. Country A had the highest rates of both recycling and composting.

Country A recycled almost half of its household waste (48%) and composted a further 18%, while most of the remainder (32%) was burned, leaving just 2% for landfill. Country C followed a similar pattern, sending only 5% to landfill, but it relied more heavily on incineration, which at 45% was its main method, ahead of recycling at 35%.

In Country B, by contrast, half of all waste was buried in landfill, although just over a quarter (27%) was recycled. Country D had by far the least sustainable profile: four-fifths of its waste ended up in landfill, and it recycled and composted only 12% and 3% respectively. Incineration was also uncommon there, accounting for a mere 5%.

*(181 words)*

## Examiner notes
- **Group countries** with similar profiles (A + C vs B + D) — the overview becomes obvious.
- Fractions vary the language: *almost half, just over a quarter, four-fifths*.
- *"least sustainable profile"* is an evaluative comparison drawn directly from the data — acceptable because it summarises the figures (high landfill, low recycling) rather than adding outside opinion.
- Check every row adds up to 100% — the examiner won't, but it helps you spot the "remainder".
