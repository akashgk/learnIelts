# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The maps illustrate the changes that have taken place in the centre of Westbury between 1995 and the present day.

Overall, the town centre has become less industrial and more focused on residents, leisure and public transport. The most significant developments are the pedestrianisation of the High Street and the conversion of the factory into housing, although the basic street layout has remained the same.

In the northern part of the town, the factory that once stood in the north-west has been converted into apartments, while the houses to the north-east are unchanged. The High Street, which formerly carried traffic in both directions, is now closed to vehicles and reserved for pedestrians. Along this street, the post office has been replaced by a café and the bank has made way for a restaurant, although the shops in the middle remain.

To the south, the open-air car park has been redeveloped as a bus station, and a multi-storey car park has been built on the western edge of the town. The park in the south-east has been reduced in size, but a playground has been added to it. The railway station is still located in the east.

## Examiner notes

- Maps need **present perfect passive**: *has been converted, has been replaced, has been built, has been reduced*.
- Organise by **area** (north → High Street → south), not by listing every change randomly.
- Mention what **stayed the same** briefly — it helps comparison.
- Use **location language**: *in the north-west, on the western edge, to the south, along this street*.
