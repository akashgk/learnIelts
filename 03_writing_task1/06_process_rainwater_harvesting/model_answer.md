# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The diagram shows how rainwater can be captured from the roof of a house, stored and treated so that it can be used for various domestic purposes.

Overall, the system involves six main stages, from the collection of rain on the roof to its distribution inside the home. A key feature is that the water follows two different paths at the end of the process, depending on whether or not it is intended for drinking.

To begin with, rain falling on the roof runs into gutters and down a pipe, where it passes through a filter that removes leaves and other debris. The first flow of water, which is likely to be the dirtiest, is then diverted to a drain, while the remainder is channelled into an underground storage tank. If the tank becomes full, excess water escapes through an overflow pipe into a soakaway in the garden.

From the tank, the water is pumped into the house. Some of it is used without further treatment to flush toilets, run the washing machine and water the garden. The rest is purified by a fine filter and an ultraviolet lamp before being supplied as drinking water to the kitchen tap.

## Examiner notes

- This diagram has a **branch** at the end. Mentioning the split in the overview shows the examiner you understood the whole system.
- Notice the mix of **active** (*rain runs into gutters, excess water escapes*) and **passive** (*is diverted, is pumped, is purified*). Use active verbs when the water "does" something naturally.
- *"which is likely to be the dirtiest"* — a reasonable inference from the label "first dirty water". Don't add facts that aren't in the diagram.
- **Relative clauses** (*where it passes through…*, *which is likely…*) are an easy way to show grammatical range.
