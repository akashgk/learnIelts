# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The bar chart compares the proportion of men and women in five age groups who exercised regularly in one country in 2023.

Overall, participation in regular exercise declined with age for both sexes. However, while men were considerably more active than women in the youngest age groups, this pattern was reversed from the age of 45 onwards.

Among 18- to 24-year-olds, more than two-thirds of men (68%) exercised regularly, compared with 55% of women. The figures for both sexes fell in the 25–34 age group, to 60% and 50% respectively, and by the 35–44 bracket the gender gap had almost disappeared, with 48% of men and 47% of women taking regular exercise.

In the older age groups, women were more active than men. In the 45–64 bracket, 44% of women exercised regularly, slightly more than the 40% of men. The lowest levels of participation were recorded among the over-65s, where just 28% of men and a third of women were regularly active. Notably, the decline across the age groups was much steeper for men, whose participation fell by 40 percentage points, than for women, whose rate dropped by 22 points.

## Examiner notes
- The key feature is the **reversal** (men more active when young, women more active when older) — put it in the overview.
- **Percentage points** vs per cent: 68% → 28% is a fall of 40 percentage points.
- *bracket, age group, the over-65s, 18- to 24-year-olds* — vary how you name the categories.
