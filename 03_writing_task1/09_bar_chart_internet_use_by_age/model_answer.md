# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The bar chart compares the average amount of time that five age groups in one country spent on the internet each day in 2012 and 2022.

Overall, daily internet use increased substantially across every age group over the decade. In both years, there was a clear inverse relationship between age and time spent online, with the youngest group using the internet the most and the oldest the least. However, the gap between older and younger users narrowed in relative terms.

In 2012, people aged 16 to 24 spent an average of 3.5 hours a day online, slightly more than the 3 hours recorded for 25- to 34-year-olds. Usage then declined with age, falling to 2.1 hours for the 35–54 group and only half an hour for those aged 65 and over.

By 2022, the figures for the two youngest groups had risen to 6.2 and 5.4 hours respectively, and the middle-aged group roughly doubled its usage to 4.1 hours. The most dramatic proportional growth, however, was among older people: those aged 55–64 went online for 3 hours a day, two and a half times the 2012 figure, while time spent online by the over-65s more than quadrupled to 2.2 hours.

## Examiner notes

- **"Inverse relationship"** is a sophisticated way to say "the older the group, the lower the figure".
- **Absolute vs relative change:** the youngest group added the most hours (+2.7), but the oldest group grew the most **proportionally** (×4.4). Pointing this out is exactly the kind of comparison that lifts Task Achievement.
- Hyphenated ages: *25- to 34-year-olds*, *the over-65s*, *those aged 55–64*.
- Don't over-write. Over 250 words is not penalised directly, but it wastes time you need for Task 2.
