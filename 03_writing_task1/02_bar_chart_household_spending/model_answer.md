# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The bar chart compares how the average household in one country divided its spending among seven categories in 1990 and 2020.

Overall, housing was the largest single expense in both years, and its share grew substantially over the period. By contrast, the proportions spent on food and clothing fell markedly, while most other categories changed only slightly.

In 1990, housing and food together accounted for almost half of all household expenditure, at 24% and 22% respectively. Thirty years later, however, the gap between them had widened considerably: housing costs rose to a third of the total budget, whereas spending on food dropped to 15%. Clothing saw an even more dramatic relative decline, halving from 10% to just 5%.

The remaining categories showed smaller variations. The share devoted to leisure increased from 9% to 12%, and health spending, although still the smallest category, almost doubled from 4% to 7%. Transport remained virtually unchanged at around 13–14%, while miscellaneous 'other' expenses decreased slightly, from 18% to 14%.

## Examiner notes

- **Overview** identifies the biggest category, the biggest increase and the biggest decreases — without numbers.
- **Grouping:** Body 1 = the big changes (housing, food, clothing). Body 2 = the smaller changes. This is better than going through the categories in chart order.
- **Percentages vocabulary:** *accounted for, a third of, halving, almost doubled, the share devoted to, respectively.*
- ⚠️ Be careful with *percentage* vs *percentage points*: housing rose by 9 percentage points (from 24% to 33%), not by 9%.

## Alternative phrases for proportions

| Number | Phrase |
|---|---|
| 25% | a quarter |
| 33% | a third / roughly one in three |
| 50% | half |
| 48% | just under half / almost half |
| 52% | just over half / a slight majority |
| 75% | three quarters / the vast majority (if very high) |
