# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The diagram illustrates the process by which chocolate is produced, from the harvesting of cacao pods to the packaging of finished bars.

Overall, there are eight main stages, which can be divided into two phases: the initial preparation of the beans in the tropical regions where they are grown, and their processing into chocolate in a factory.

The process begins when ripe cacao pods are harvested by hand using machetes. The pods are then split open, and the beans, together with the surrounding pulp, are removed. Next, the beans are left to ferment in boxes for five to seven days, after which they are spread out and dried in the sun for one to two weeks. Once dry, they are packed into sacks and shipped to chocolate factories.

At the factory, the beans are roasted at temperatures of between 120 and 150°C, and their shells are removed, leaving small pieces known as nibs. These are ground into a thick paste called cocoa liquor, which is then mixed with sugar, milk powder and cocoa butter and stirred for a long period in a refining process called conching. Finally, the chocolate is tempered by being heated and cooled, poured into moulds and packaged as bars.

## Examiner notes

- Technical terms are explained: *small pieces known as nibs, a thick paste called cocoa liquor*.
- *tempered by being heated and cooled* — passive gerund, good grammatical range.
