# Model answer (Band 8–9)

The line graph illustrates changes in the number of overseas students studying at universities in Australia, Canada and Germany over a twenty-year period from 2000.

Overall, international enrolments rose considerably in all three countries. Australia overtook Germany to become the most popular destination by the end of the period, while Canada, despite consistently attracting the fewest students, recorded the most dramatic growth.

In 2000, Germany was the leading destination, with 175,000 international students, compared with 105,000 in Australia and just 60,000 in Canada. Over the following decade, the figure for Australia climbed steeply, more than doubling to 240,000 by 2010, whereas German enrolments remained relatively stable, fluctuating between 175,000 and 190,000. Canada saw only modest growth during this time, reaching 95,000.

From 2010 onwards, all three countries experienced an upward trend. Australia continued to lead, finishing the period at 330,000. Germany's numbers recovered strongly, rising to 300,000 in 2020. The most striking change, however, occurred in Canada, where enrolments surged from 95,000 to 255,000, meaning the country had more than quadrupled its intake since 2000.

## Why this answer scores highly

| Criterion | What the answer does |
|---|---|
| **Task Achievement** | Clear **overview** in paragraph 2 (overall trend + the two most important features). Every key figure is reported accurately, and comparisons are made throughout. No opinions or causes are invented. |
| **Coherence & Cohesion** | Four-paragraph structure: introduction → overview → 2000–2010 → 2010–2020. Grouping by **time period** makes comparisons easy. Linkers vary: *whereas, however, while, meaning*. |
| **Lexical Resource** | Paraphrases the prompt (*international students → overseas students; between 2000 and 2020 → over a twenty-year period*). Precise trend vocabulary: *climbed steeply, fluctuating, surged, recovered strongly*. |
| **Grammar** | Mix of simple and complex sentences, participle clause (*meaning the country had…*), past perfect, comparatives (*the most dramatic*). |

## Structure you can reuse

1. **Introduction (1 sentence):** paraphrase what the chart shows — what, where, when.
2. **Overview (2 sentences):** the main trend + the biggest/most noticeable feature. *No numbers needed here.*
3. **Body 1:** the first period OR the first group of data, with figures.
4. **Body 2:** the second period OR group, with figures and comparisons.

## Useful language — describing trends

| Upward | Downward | Little change | Speed / size |
|---|---|---|---|
| rise, climb, increase, grow, surge, soar | fall, drop, decline, decrease, plunge | remain stable, level off, plateau, fluctuate | dramatically, sharply, steeply, steadily, gradually, slightly, marginally |

**Common mistakes to avoid**
- ❌ Writing the overview at the end with no heading — the examiner must find it easily. Put it second.
- ❌ Listing every number year by year. Select the significant ones.
- ❌ *"The number of students increased from 105 to 330."* → Add the unit: *105,000 to 330,000*.
- ❌ Giving reasons (*"because of better visa rules"*). Task 1 describes data only.
