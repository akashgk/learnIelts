## Questions 1–6 · Matching (headings, information, features, endings)

> **Strategy:** Read the paragraph first, decide its main idea in your own words, then look for the heading. Headings summarise the WHOLE paragraph, not one detail in it. Cross out headings as you use them.

**1. ii** — The whole paragraph explains that building materials absorb heat and release it slowly, and that urban canyons trap heat, so cities 'cool far more slowly at night'.
  
  *Evidence:* “The result is that cities cool far more slowly at night than rural areas”

**2. iv** — The paragraph describes evapotranspiration (a natural cooling process) and what happens when forests and fields are replaced by roads.
  
  *Evidence:* “When forests and fields are replaced by roads and car parks, this natural cooling mechanism disappears.”
  
  *Trap:* Air-conditioning is mentioned, but only as a comparison — heading vii is a distractor.

**3. v** — The paragraph lists consequences: mortality (health), electricity demand (energy) and ground-level ozone (air quality).
  
  *Evidence:* “The consequences extend well beyond discomfort.”

**4. i** — Cool roofs and light paving are the solution; the final sentence notes that reflective road surfaces 'can create glare for drivers'.
  
  *Evidence:* “reflective road surfaces can create glare for drivers”

**5. vi** — Green infrastructure is presented with its benefits (evapotranspiration, shade) and then its drawbacks ('not without drawbacks').
  
  *Evidence:* “However, green infrastructure is not without drawbacks.”

**6. iii** — Researchers argue heat should be treated 'as a public health issue' and the paragraph ends by calling it 'a question of social fairness'.
  
  *Evidence:* “not only an environmental challenge but also a question of social fairness”

## Questions 7–10 · True / False / Not Given

> **Strategy:** FALSE means the passage says the opposite. NOT GIVEN means the passage simply does not tell you. Never use your own knowledge.

**7. FALSE** — Howard blamed the burning of fuel, but paragraph B says 'the main culprit is the material from which cities are built', and paragraph C says waste heat contributes less than surfaces.
  
  *Evidence:* “Howard attributed the difference to the burning of fuel, but later research has shown that the explanation is considerably more complex.”

**8. TRUE** — Paragraph B directly states this. 'Strongest after dark' = 'strongest at night'.
  
  *Evidence:* “the heat island effect is typically strongest after dark rather than at midday”

**9. FALSE** — Paragraph C says waste heat contributes LESS than surfaces and loss of greenery.
  
  *Evidence:* “in most cities this contributes less than the properties of surfaces and the loss of greenery”

**10. NOT GIVEN** — Paragraph D discusses mortality in Paris in 2003 but says nothing about any regulations introduced afterwards.
  
  *Trap:* The topic (Paris, 2003) is in the passage, which tempts you to answer TRUE or FALSE — but the specific claim is never addressed.
