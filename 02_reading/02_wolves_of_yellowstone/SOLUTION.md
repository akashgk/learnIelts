## Questions 1–4 · Matching (headings, information, features, endings)

> **Strategy:** 'Matching information' questions are NOT in passage order. Underline the key noun in each statement (e.g. 'reason', 'example', 'reference'), then skim paragraph by paragraph.

**1. E** — Paragraph E ends by explaining there was no 'control' Yellowstone for comparison.
  
  *Evidence:* “there was no 'control' Yellowstone without wolves with which the real park could be compared”

**2. A** — Rangers culled elk to control their numbers before abandoning the practice.
  
  *Evidence:* “Park rangers attempted to control elk numbers by culling”

**3. D** — 'documentaries and popular videos watched by millions' = a non-scientific audience.
  
  *Evidence:* “featuring in documentaries and popular videos watched by millions of people”

**4. B** — 'legal challenges from farming organisations' = opposition.
  
  *Evidence:* “legal challenges from farming organisations”

## Questions 5–8 · Multiple choice (one answer)

> **Strategy:** Locate the part of the passage, then eliminate options: one is usually true but irrelevant, one is the opposite, one mixes two details.

**5. B** — 'did not just reduce the number of elk; they also changed the elk's behaviour' — avoiding valleys and grazing less in one place.
  
  *Trap:* C — beavers increased INDIRECTLY via trees, not directly because of wolves.

**6. A** — Definition at the end of paragraph C: 'a chain of indirect effects'.
  
  *Evidence:* “Ecologists call such a chain of indirect effects a 'trophic cascade'.”

**7. C** — Overgrazing lowered the water table, 'leaving roots unable to reach moisture'.
  
  *Trap:* A — the passage says elk can graze in risky areas at midday, but this explains why elk did not change behaviour, not why willows stayed short.

**8. C** — 'natural systems rarely have a single cause and effect'.
  
  *Trap:* A is directly contradicted: 'None of this means that the reintroduction was a failure.'
