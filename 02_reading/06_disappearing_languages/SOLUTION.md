## Questions 1–4 · Multiple choice (one answer)

**1. B** — 'Many of these smaller languages are not being learned by children, which linguists regard as the clearest sign that a language is in danger.'
  
  *Trap:* A — small size is mentioned, but it is not called 'the clearest sign'.

**2. C** — 'Languages have always died … What is unprecedented … is the current rate of loss.'
  
  *Trap:* D — the writer mentions 'countless unrecorded tongues', the opposite.

**3. B** — They illustrate the scientific argument: each language is 'a distinct solution', showing the 'full range of possible grammatical structures'.

**4. C** — 'each depended on strong commitment from the communities themselves'.

## Questions 5–9 · Yes / No / Not Given

**5. NO** — The writer says they do so 'often for entirely understandable reasons'.

**6. NO** — 'it is rarely translated in full'.

**7. YES** — 'These are serious points.' The writer then disagrees with their assumption, but accepts they are serious.

**8. YES** — 'bilingualism is the historical norm for most of humanity'.

**9. NOT GIVEN** — Welsh has 'stabilised'; nothing is said about current versus historical numbers.
  
  *Trap:* 'Stabilised' ≠ 'grown to a record level'.

## Questions 10–14 · Matching (headings, information, features, endings)

> **Strategy:** With a word box, decide the grammar of the gap first (noun, adjective, plural?) — this usually eliminates half the options.

**10. C** — 'detailed understanding of local plants, animals, weather and medicine' = environment.

**11. A** — 'A third argument … concerns identity.'

**12. B** — 'a shared language reduces the costs of trade and communication'.

**13. E** — 'language nests' — preschools.
  
  *Trap:* H — universities are not mentioned.

**14. D** — 'the decline of a language is not an inevitable process'.
  
  *Trap:* G 'reversible' would reverse the meaning: the writer believes decline IS reversible.
