## Questions 1–5 · Completion (sentence / note / table / form / summary)

> **Strategy:** Notes follow passage order. 'A NUMBER' means digits count separately from words: '15' is one number, so '15 degrees' = one number + one word = allowed.

**1. Pole Star** — Paragraph 1: 'the position of the Pole Star at night'.

**2. 15 degrees, fifteen degrees** — Paragraph 1: 'each hour of difference … corresponds to 15 degrees of longitude'.

**3. humidity** — Paragraph 1: 'changes in temperature and humidity caused metal parts to expand'.

**4. scurvy** — Paragraph 2: 'while their crews died of scurvy'.

**5. degree** — Paragraph 2: 'to within half a degree'. Notice the note already gives 'half a', so you only need one word — copying 'half a degree' into the gap would make the sentence wrong.

## Questions 6–9 · True / False / Not Given

**6. FALSE** — The Act was passed 'partly in response to such disasters' — 'partly' contradicts 'the only reason'.
  
  *Trap:* Watch absolute words in statements: only, always, never, all.

**7. FALSE** — Paragraph 3: 'The method was sound in principle'. The difficulty was practical (tables, calculation time).

**8. TRUE** — Paragraph 4: 'building his first clocks almost entirely from wood'.

**9. NOT GIVEN** — France is never mentioned. The passage mentions later chronometers 'based on Harrison's principles' but nothing about French copies.

## Questions 10–13 · Matching (headings, information, features, endings)

> **Strategy:** Scan for each name and read the sentences around EVERY mention — the information about one person is often spread across paragraphs.

**10. C** — Maskelyne 'published a nautical almanac that made lunar calculations considerably quicker'.

**11. D** — 'King George III, who personally tested one of Harrison's watches'.

**12. B** — The Board 'doubted whether a single successful watch proved that the method could be reproduced'.
  
  *Trap:* Maskelyne was a member of the Board, but the passage attributes this doubt to the Board as a whole.

**13. A** — Harrison introduced 'a strip made from two different metals that compensated for changes in temperature'.
