# Vocabulary — Family & Children

| Word | Type | Meaning | Example | Collocations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **upbringing** | n | the way a child is raised | *A strict upbringing can shape a person's values.* | a strict / happy upbringing |
| **nuclear family** | n | parents and their children only | *The nuclear family is now the norm in cities.* | the traditional nuclear family |
| **extended family** | n | relatives beyond parents and children | *She grew up surrounded by her extended family.* | live with the extended family |
| **sibling** | n | a brother or sister | *Siblings often compete for attention.* | older / younger sibling |
| **role model** | n | a person others try to copy | *Parents are children's first role models.* | a positive role model |
| **discipline** | n/v | training to obey rules; to punish | *Consistent discipline helps children feel secure.* | impose / maintain discipline |
| **generation gap** | n | differences between older and younger people | *Technology has widened the generation gap.* | bridge the generation gap |
| **single-parent** | adj | with only one parent | *Single-parent families may need extra support.* | single-parent household / family |
| **childcare** | n | looking after children while parents work | *The high cost of childcare keeps many parents at home.* | affordable childcare |
| **overprotective** | adj | protecting someone too much | *Overprotective parents may limit independence.* | overprotective parents |
| **bond** | n/v | a close connection | *Shared meals strengthen family bonds.* | strengthen / form a bond |
| **peer pressure** | n | influence from people of the same age | *Teenagers often give in to peer pressure.* | give in to / resist peer pressure |

> **How to study:** cover the *Meaning* column and test yourself; then write your own sentence for each word using one of its collocations. Do the exercises in `QUESTIONS.md` and check `SOLUTION.md`.
